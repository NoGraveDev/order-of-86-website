## EDITOR'S NOTE
*From the Tower Archive, Prophetic Folio, compiled by Brother Deep-Oracle, Tidewatch, 1,090 YFH*

Not all prophets are Wizards. Not all Wizards are prophets. The ability to see the future is separate from the ability to channel magic. Some of the most accurate prophets in Pawtheon history have been unmagical dogs.

[Ed. Note] I have profiled seven notable seers, from the legendary to the notorious. Each represents a different approach to prophecy.

## I. SERA THE SILENT (c. 600–700 YFH)

### Profile

Sera was a pre-Howl Listener who survived the evolutionary surge without gaining magical ability. She was blind from birth and deaf in one ear. She claimed that her remaining ear could hear "the wind's memory" — echoes of events that had not yet occurred.

### Method

Sera did not scry. She did not meditate. She simply sat in windy places and listened. Her prophecies were delivered as riddles, often in the form of questions:

> "What has no voice but will silence all songs?"
> "What walks on four legs, then two, then none, then all?"
> "What is borrowed and never returned, yet must be given freely?"

### Accuracy

Sera made approximately 200 prophecies. Of these, 47 have been verified as accurate. 89 were too vague to verify. 64 were clearly wrong.

Her most famous prophecy — the First Howl Prophecy — was ignored for 400 years before the Great Silence made it relevant.

### Legacy

The Mute claims Sera as their spiritual ancestor. The Deep Order claims her as a pre-Wizard prophet. The Chorus Council officially considers her "a historical curiosity."

## II. POOL-SEER THE HALF-BLIND (850–910 YFH)

### Profile

Pool-Seer was a Deep Wizard who specialized in contaminated-water scrying. While other Deep Wizards used clean pools, Pool-Seer believed that polluted water — water mixed with rot, ash, or emotional residue — produced clearer visions.

### Method

Pool-Seer scried in a pool contaminated with Mud Rot residue. The contamination gave him headaches, hallucinations, and eventually permanent blindness in one eye. But it also gave him visions that other prophets could not match.

He recorded his prophecies in wax-sealed containers, to be opened only after his death. This was not vanity. He believed that prophecies were dangerous when known too soon.

### Accuracy

Pool-Seer made 31 sealed prophecies. Of these, 23 have been opened. 18 were accurate. 5 were wrong.

His most famous prophecy — the Wrong Note Prophecy — was opened in 910 YFH. The first Wrong Note was reported in 912 YFH.

### Legacy

The Deep Order is divided on Pool-Seer. Some consider him a genius who sacrificed his sight for truth. Others consider him a cautionary tale about the dangers of contaminated scrying.

The Tidewatch no longer allows scrying in polluted water.

## III. THREAD-WEAVER THE FRACTURED (950–1,030 YFH)

### Profile

Thread-Weaver was a Dream Wizard who attempted to heal a shattered dream-thread between two dogs who had been enemies. The healing went wrong. Thread-Weaver's mind was pulled into the fracture, and a piece of her remained trapped there.

She spent the last ten years of her life in the Dream Spire's care ward, speaking only in prophecies. Her caregivers believed she was not seeing the future but "remembering" it from her fractured perspective.

### Method

Thread-Weaver did not control her prophecies. They emerged spontaneously, often during sleep. Her caregivers recorded everything she said. Approximately 60% of her utterances were gibberish. 30% were poetic but unverifiable. 10% were clearly prophetic.

### Accuracy

Of her verifiable prophecies, 70% were accurate. This is the highest accuracy rate of any prophet in Pawtheon history.

Her most famous prophecy — the Pack-Breaker Prophecy — predicted the Loyalty Wars with eerie precision.

### Legacy

The Dream Order uses Thread-Weaver as a teaching example: both of the dangers of failed thread-healing and of the potential rewards. Her care ward room is preserved as a shrine.

## IV. ASHPAW THE RENOUNCED (1,050–1,130 YFH)

### Profile

Ashpaw was not a prophet in the traditional sense. He was a Flame Wizard who renounced his hat and founded the Mute. But his pamphlets contain passages that function as prophecies — predictions about the future of magic that have proven increasingly accurate.

### Method

Ashpaw did not scry or dream. He reasoned. He observed patterns in magical decay and extrapolated trends. His "prophecies" were actually educated guesses based on data.

> "Magic that must be borrowed will eventually be refused."
> "A dog who needs a hat to be loyal will eventually lose both hat and loyalty."
> "The Hollow does not hurry. The Hollow waits. And what waits always arrives."

### Accuracy

Ashpaw was not always right. He predicted the permanent end of magic by 1,150 YFH. That did not happen. But his broader predictions — about the Mute's growth, about the Orders' increasing rigidity, about the civilian population's declining trust in Wizards — have all proven accurate.

### Legacy

The Mute considers Ashpaw their founder and prophet. The Chorus Council considers him a heretic and statistician. The Arcane Order considers him "the most accurate non-magical predictor in Pawtheon history."

## V. LISTENER DEEP-CAVE (960–1,020 YFH)

### Profile

Listener Deep-Cave was not a Wizard. He was a member of the Listener Order — a monastic group that sits in silence and simply listens. He sat in a cave beneath the Forge Spire for one year.

### Method

Deep-Cave did not seek visions. He sought sounds. On the third day of his return speech, he described hearing "a bass note that has been playing since before the First Howl." He claimed the note was "patient" and "waiting."

Six months later, he went deaf. He claimed it was not a loss but a translation: he had heard the bass note so clearly that all other sounds became irrelevant.

### Accuracy

Deep-Cave made no explicit prophecies. But his description of the bass note is widely interpreted as the first recorded awareness of the Hollow.

### Legacy

The Listener Order considers Deep-Cave their greatest member. The Mute considers him a prophet of the Hollow. The Deep Order considers him "a dog who heard something and then stopped hearing everything else."

## VI. THE GRAY DOG

### Profile

The Gray Dog is not a prophet. He does not scry. He does not dream. He does not listen to the wind.

But he knows things.

Witnesses consistently report that the Gray Dog arrives in places just before important events occur. He is never surprised. When the Great Silence began, he was already on the unnamed mountain, looking at the dark moons. When the Loyalty Wars ended, he was already sitting between the battle lines.

### Method

Unknown. The Gray Dog has never explained how he knows what he knows. When asked, he says: "I walk a lot. I see a lot."

### Accuracy

Perfect. The Gray Dog has never been wrong about anything he has chosen to comment on. The problem is that he rarely comments.

### Legacy

Every prophet in Pawtheon is compared to the Gray Dog. None have matched him. The Deep Order has a standing offer of permanent residence at the Tidewatch for any prophet who achieves 100% accuracy. The offer has never been claimed.

## VII. THE PUP-WHO-HUMS (1,180 YFH–present)

### Profile

The Pup-Who-Hums is not a single dog. It is a title given to any pup who hums before they howl. These pups are considered prophetically sensitive.

The most famous current Pup-Who-Hums is a Classic from the Violet Highlands, born in 1,198 YFH. She has not yet been sorted into an Order. She hums constantly — not songs, but single tones that shift gradually over hours.

### Method

The Pup-Who-Hums does not consciously prophesy. The tones she produces are not meaningful in themselves. But Deep Wizards who record and analyze the tones have found that they sometimes match the frequencies of future events.

### Accuracy

Too early to tell. The current Pup-Who-Hums has produced approximately 500 hours of recorded humming. Of these, 12 segments have been matched to subsequent events. The match rate is low but statistically significant.

### Legacy

The Chorus Council monitors all Pups-Who-Hums. The Mute recruits them. The Dream Order studies their dreams. The Arcane Order analyzes their frequencies.

The Pup-Who-Hums does not care. She simply hums.

## PROPHECY METHODS

### Water Scrying
The most common Deep Order method. The prophet gazes into still water and interprets the patterns of light and shadow. Accuracy depends on the prophet's skill and the water's purity.

### Dream-Walking
The Dream Order method. The prophet enters a controlled sleep and navigates the collective unconscious, searching for future-images. Dangerous: the prophet may become lost.

### Wind-Listening
The Listener Order method. The prophet sits in a windy place and listens for "memory-tones" — frequencies that carry information from the future. Requires extreme patience.

### Pattern Recognition
The Arcane Order method. The prophet analyzes historical data, astronomical patterns, and social trends to predict future events. Not magical. Simply mathematical.

### Blood-Divination
The Blood Tides method. The prophet reads patterns in their own blood. Banned by all Orders. Considered unsanitary and psychologically destabilizing.

### Rot-Communion
The Rot Gardeners method. The prophet bonds with a Rot colony and interprets its growth patterns as prophecies. Extremely dangerous. The Rot may consume the prophet.
