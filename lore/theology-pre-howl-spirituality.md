## I. THE SILENT GODS

### Belief

Before the Gray Dog howled, the seven moons were called the Silent Gods.

They were not worshipped as creators. They were worshipped as witnesses. The Old Packs believed the moons had been watching since before dogs existed, since before the world had grass, since before the first wolf chose loyalty over hunger. The Silent Gods did not intervene. They did not speak. They simply watched, night after night, and their watching was enough.

A moonless night was considered ill-omened. Not because the Silent Gods were angry, but because they were absent. An unwatched dog was a dog who might stray. An unwatched pack was a pack that might forget what loyalty meant.

### Practice

**The Moon-Watch:** Every pack designated a Moon-Watcher — usually the oldest dog, the one who slept least. The Moon-Watcher's duty was to sit awake while the pack slept, looking up at the Silent Gods, ensuring that someone was always watching the watchers.

When a Moon-Watcher died, the pack held a vigil. They did not howl. Howling was reserved for communication. For the Moon-Watcher, they were silent. They simply sat and looked up, as the dead dog had looked up, until dawn.

[Pre-Howl] Archaeological evidence: burial sites oriented toward the moon's path. Skeletons placed on their backs, facing upward.

## II. THE LISTENERS

### Belief

Some dogs heard things others did not. A branch cracking in a pattern. Wind whistling through stone in a rhythm. The sound of water underground. These dogs were called Listeners.

The Old Packs did not believe Listeners were magical. They believed Listeners were simply dogs who paid closer attention. A Listener was not chosen by the gods. A Listener was a dog who chose to listen.

### Practice

**The Listening Vigil:** A Listener would sit in a chosen place — a cave, a hollow tree, a riverbank — and listen for seven days. They did not hunt. They did not sleep deeply. They simply listened.

On the seventh day, the Listener returned to the pack and reported what they had heard. Sometimes it was practical: "The river is rising." Sometimes it was metaphorical: "The stones are arguing." Sometimes it was impossible to verify: "The wind remembers a forest that does not exist."

The pack did not treat the Listener as a prophet. They treated the Listener as a scout. Some reports were useful. Some were not. The Listener was judged by accuracy, not by mystique.

[Pre-Howl] Archaeological evidence: caves with worn stone where dogs sat for long periods. No tools. No offerings. Just worn stone.

### Connection to Modern Orders

The modern Deep Order claims descent from the Listeners. They are not wrong. The first Deep Wizards were dogs who had been Listeners before the Second Howl, dogs whose ears were already trained to hear what others missed. When Deepwell's blue light found them, they were ready.

The Mute also claims descent from the Listeners, arguing that true listening requires silence, not magic. Both claims are partly true. Neither is the whole truth.

## III. THE LONG RUN

### Belief

The Old Packs believed in an afterlife called the Long Run.

When a dog died, their spirit joined a pack that ran forever across an endless plain. The plain had no prey, no predators, no boundaries. The dogs ran not because they were hunting, but because running was what dogs did. They ran together. They never tired. They never hungered. They simply ran, and the running was enough.

A dog who had been loyal in life ran in the center of the pack, surrounded by packmates. A dog who had been solitary ran at the edge, but the pack never outpaced them. However fast they ran, the pack ran with them.

### Practice

**The Scent-Trail to the Dead:** When a dog died, the pack laid a trail of scent from the body to the highest point they could reach — a hill, a cliff, a tall tree. The scent-trail was made from the dead dog's favorite things: the bush they marked every morning, the stone they scratched, the bedding they slept on.

The belief was that the spirit would follow its own scent to the high place, and from there, see the Long Run plain stretching out below. Once they saw it, they would know where to go.

[Pre-Howl] Archaeological evidence: elevated burial platforms. Bodies placed on raised wooden structures (now decayed, but post-holes remain) with scattered organic material around the base.

**The Circle Howl:** This was the only ritual howl of the Old Packs. When a pup was born, the pack formed a circle around the mother and howled in rotation. Each dog took one note, low to high, creating a chord that rose and fell. The chord was not magic. It was simply welcome.

[Pack Memory] Some Wizards report that the Circle Howl, performed without magic, still produces a faint resonance in the Loyalty Network. This has never been verified.

## IV. BONE-KEEPING

### Belief

The skulls of alpha dogs were preserved. Not as trophies. As guidance.

The Old Packs believed that an alpha's wisdom lingered in the bone, particularly in the hollows of the eye sockets. A pack facing a difficult decision would bring the skulls of past alphas into the circle and describe the problem. Then they would wait.

They did not expect the skulls to speak. They expected the memory of the alpha's judgment to guide their own. A dog who had known a wise alpha would ask: "What would Old Scar have done?" The skull was a focus for memory, not a source of supernatural advice.

### Practice

**The Bone Niche:** Every Old Pack den had a bone niche — a hollow in stone or earth where skulls were kept. The niche was not decorated. It was not sacred in the way a temple is sacred. It was simply a place for the dead to remain part of the conversation.

When a new alpha was chosen, they spent their first night in the bone niche, sleeping among the skulls of their predecessors. This was not a supernatural ritual. It was a reminder: you are temporary. Make your decisions count.

[Pre-Howl] Archaeological evidence: caves with natural or carved hollows containing dog skulls, often arranged in rows. The skulls show no tool marks, suggesting they were cleaned by scavengers (intentionally placed for natural defleshing) rather than processed by the pack.

## V. THE PACK OATH

### Belief

Loyalty was not assumed. It was sworn.

When a pup reached adulthood (usually their first winter), they stood before the pack and spoke the Pack Oath. The exact wording varied by pack, but the structure was always the same:

1. **The Looking Back:** "I remember the dogs who fed me when I could not feed myself."
2. **The Looking Forward:** "I will feed the dogs who cannot feed themselves."
3. **The Looking Around:** "I will run with this pack, not because I must, but because I choose to."
4. **The Looking Up:** "I will watch the Silent Gods, so the pack is never unwatched."

### Practice

The Pack Oath was spoken, not howled. Howling was for the pack. Speaking was for the individual. The oath was the moment a pup became a dog.

Some modern Wizards — particularly in the Dream Order — have tried to revive the Pack Oath as a binding ritual for dream-threads. The Mute practices it in its original form, without magic. The Chorus considers the Mute version "theatrical nostalgia."

[Pack Memory] The Gray Dog reportedly knew the Pack Oath. When he founded the Starter Lands, he required every apprentice to speak it before entering. He did not explain why. He simply said: "You should know what you are promising."

## VI. THE COMING OF MAGIC

### The Old Pack Response

The First Howl did not convert every dog immediately. For the first generation, many packs rejected the changes. They considered the evolving dogs — the ones who stood on two legs, who felt the Current — to be cursed. Some packs drove the changed dogs out. Some packs killed them.

Other packs embraced the change. They considered the evolving dogs to be "touched by the Silent Gods" — finally answered after eternity of watching. These packs became the first magical communities.

Most packs simply adapted. They did not worship the magic. They used it. A dog who could start a fire with a thought was useful in winter. A dog who could sense the Loyalty Network was useful for finding lost packmates. The Old Pack Ways did not disappear overnight. They were simply... upgraded.

By 400 YFH, the Old Pack Ways were practiced only in the most isolated regions. By 600 YFH, they were considered folklore. By 800 YFH, most dogs believed the Old Packs had never existed.

The Mute rediscovered them in 1,100 YFH. Whether they found the true Old Pack Ways or invented a version that suited their doctrine is a matter of scholarly debate.

## VII. THE UNCHANGED PLACES

Some places in Pawtheon still practice elements of the Old Pack Ways:

**The Frosthollow Expanse:** The northernmost Husky packs never fully adopted magic. They use it for survival — warming dens, finding prey — but they still preserve bone niches and perform the Circle Howl for new pups.

**The Deepwood Heartwood:** The oldest trees in the Deepwood are said to contain pack-memory from before the First Howl. The Collie Wardens — a Wild Order sub-order — maintain these trees and claim to hear whispers from the Old Packs in their rustling leaves.

**The Shadowmire Depths:** The Zombie dogs practice a corrupted version of bone-keeping, preserving not just skulls but entire skeletons. Whether this is a genuine continuation of the Old Pack Ways or a post-Rot innovation is unknown.
