ORDER HIERARCHIES

Ranks, Initiation, and Training in the Six Orders

[Edition note] Revised1200YFH edition. Original compiler dates below identify earlier textual layers; later events and citations belong to this revision. Canon Contract continuity settlement governs literal chronology, seat counts and physical rules.

EDITOR'S NOTE

From the Tower Archive, Institutional Folio, compiled by Brother Violet-Record, 1,020 YFH

Each Order has its own structure, its own titles, and its own path from apprentice to master. What follows is the standard hierarchy as recognized by the Chorus Council. Individual Towers may have local variations.

[Ed. Note] I have recorded the official structure. I have also recorded the unofficial reality. The two are rarely the same.

--- Brother Violet-Record

I. THE CHORUS COUNCIL

Overview

The Chorus Council is the overarching body that coordinates all six Orders. It was founded in 600 YFH, two hundred years after the Second Howl, when the Orders realized they needed a neutral arbiter to resolve disputes.

The Council does not govern. It mediates. It has no army, no treasury, and no power to enforce its decisions. Its authority comes entirely from the respect the Orders have for it. When the Council speaks, the Orders listen. Usually.

Structure

The Seven Voices:

  • One representative from each of the six Orders
  • The Seventh Voice is the Wanderer, who attends when he chooses and speaks only when he chooses

The Seventh Voice has never cast a vote. He has never needed to. When the Gray Dog speaks, the debate ends.

The Seat: The Chorus Council meets at the Concordance Spire, a neutral tower built in the Starter Lands. The Spire has no resonant tuning. It is simply a place to talk.

The Trial by Fire

Every Flame Wizard must undergo the Trial by Fire to advance from Ember to Flamekeeper. The trial is simple: the Wizard stands in the central forge while the Highforge raises the temperature to the maximum safe level. The Wizard must maintain a protective flame shield for one hour.

The trial kills approximately 3% of participants. The Flame Order considers this acceptable. "Fire that cannot be controlled is not fire," the Highforge says. "It is merely burning."

The Ember Oath

"I will burn what needs burning. I will warm what needs warming. I will not set fire to a dog I have not first warned. I will not let my flame go out while a packmate still shivers. I will remember that the hottest flame is the one that knows when to stop."

The Cleansing Requirement

To become one of the Pure, a Radiant Wizard must personally cleanse a site infected by the Great Rot. This is dangerous. The Rot consumes light as eagerly as it consumes flesh. Approximately 15% of cleansing missions result in the Wizard's corruption.

The Radiant Order considers this a sacred risk. "Light that does not enter darkness is merely brightness," the Highseer says.

The Revelation Oath

"I will show what is true, even when the truth burns. I will not blind a dog with light they are not ready for. I will illuminate the path, not force the walking. I will remember that the sun rises for all dogs, not only the worthy."

The Accuracy Requirement

The Deep Order is unique in requiring proof of ability. To advance from Current to Poolkeeper, a Wizard must produce a prophecy that comes true. To advance from Poolkeeper to Tided, they must produce a prophecy that comes true AND that no other prophet predicted.

This creates intense competition. Some Deep Wizards hoard their visions, releasing them only when they are certain no one else has seen the same future. The Tidewatch archives are full of "locked prophecies" — predictions sealed in wax, to be opened only after the event occurs.

The Depth Oath

"I will see what is coming, even when I wish to look away. I will not drown a dog in futures they cannot swim through. I will warn before I predict, and predict before I judge. I will remember that the deepest water is the water that looks still."

The Heartwood Selection

The Highroot is not elected by dogs. The Heartwood tree — the ancient tree at the center of the Spire — selects the Highroot by directing a specific root to touch the candidate's paw. No one knows how the tree decides. Some Highroots have been young and radical. Others have been old and conservative. The tree has its own logic.

The current Highroot, Heartwood the Rooted, was selected at age 23 — the youngest in history. She has served for 67 years and shows no sign of aging.

The Growth Oath

"I will grow what needs growing. I will prune what needs pruning. I will not force a seed to bloom before its season. I will not let a grove die while my paws still hold water. I will remember that the tallest tree was once a seed that refused to stop."

Peer Review System

The Arcane Order is the only Order that elects its leader by peer review rather than seniority or combat. The Highthink is chosen by a vote of all Archivists, based on the candidate's published research. This creates a culture where young Wizards can rise quickly if their work is groundbreaking.

The current Highthink, Violet-Think the Cautious, is a compromise candidate. Her research is solid but not revolutionary. She was elected because the two leading candidates — one a radical dimensional theorist, one a conservative temporal historian — hated each other so much that they agreed on her as a third option.

The Knowledge Oath

"I will seek what is hidden, even when the hiding was done for good reason. I will not use knowledge to harm a dog who cannot understand it. I will share what I find, but only with those ready to receive it. I will remember that some doors are closed because what is behind them is not ready to be seen."

The Dreamwoven Requirement

To become one of the Dreamwoven, a Wizard must heal a shattered dream-thread. Dream-threads are the oneiroic bonds between dogs. They can be damaged by trauma, betrayal, or magical overload. A shattered thread is painful for both parties — a constant sense of loss, like a missing limb that still aches.

Healing a shattered thread requires the Wizard to enter both dogs' dreams simultaneously and re-weave the connection. This is dangerous. If the Wizard loses focus, they can become trapped between the two minds, unable to wake.

Three Dream Wizards have become permanent Sleepers — bodies in comas, minds wandering the collective unconscious — after failed thread-healing attempts.

The Bond Oath

"I will feel what my packmate feels, even when their feelings hurt me. I will not force a bond where none exists. I will strengthen what is weak, and let go of what is broken. I will remember that the strongest thread is the one that can stretch without snapping."

VIII. THE SORTING CEREMONY

Overview

When a dog discovers they can feel the Current — usually in adolescence, around 2-3 years of age — they are brought to the Starter Lands for the Sorting Ceremony.

The ceremony is simple. The dog stands in the Center Circle, a neutral zone where no moon's influence dominates. The six Order representatives stand at the circle's edge, each tuned to their moon's frequency. The dog is asked to close their eyes and listen.

Usually, one moon's frequency resonates more strongly than the others. The dog is invited to join that Order. Occasionally, two moons resonate equally. These dogs are called Cross-Tones and are given a choice. Very rarely, no moon resonates. These dogs are called Mutes — not because they join the Mute faction, but because they are magically silent. They are sent home to live as civilians.

The Gray Dog's Exception

The Gray Dog does not participate in the Sorting Ceremony. He watches from a distance. Occasionally, a Cross-Tone dog will feel a seventh frequency — faint, diffuse, belonging to no single moon. These dogs are brought to the Gray Dog. He speaks with them. Sometimes he invites them to walk with him for a time. Sometimes he sends them back to the Ceremony to choose.

No dog has ever been sorted into the Wanderer's "Order." The Gray Dog refuses to have one.

IX. RENUNCIATION

The Process

A Wizard may renounce their hat at any time. The process is simple: the Wizard brings their hat to their Order's Tower, places it on the Hook of Return, and speaks the Words of Release:

"I return what was borrowed. I release what was given. I am a dog, not a moon. Let me be ordinary."

The hat is then burned. The Wizard is stripped of their rank and title. They become a civilian.

Consequences

Renounced Wizards retain the ability to feel the Current, but they can no longer shape it. Most report a persistent "phantom magic" — the sensation of power that is no longer there. Some compare it to losing a limb.

Renounced Wizards are not shunned by society. They are pitied. Some find meaningful lives as teachers, counselors, or historians. Others join the Mute. A small number — approximately 5% — attempt to reclaim their magic by stealing another Wizard's hat. This never works. A hat bonded to one dog will not function for another.
