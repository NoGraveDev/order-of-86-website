PROPHECIES AND OMENS

What the Future Whispers to the Present

[Edition note] Revised1200YFH edition. Original compiler dates below identify earlier textual layers; later events and citations belong to this revision. Canon Contract continuity settlement governs literal chronology, seat counts and physical rules.

EDITOR'S NOTE

From the Tower Archive, Prophetic Folio, compiled by Sister Deep-Seer, Tidewatch, 1,100 YFH

Prophecy in Pawtheon is not supernatural. It is pattern recognition. The Deep Order believes that the moons' frequencies create ripples in time, like stones dropped in a pond. A skilled prophet can read the ripples and predict where the next stone will fall.

[Ed. Note] I have recorded three major prophecies, each with multiple interpretations. I have also recorded the natural and human omens that dogs use to read the future.

The prophecies are not fate. They are warnings. Whether they come true depends on what dogs choose to do after hearing them.

--- Sister Deep-Seer

I. THE FIRST HOWL PROPHECY

The Prophecy

"When the Silent Ones rise and the Hats go dark,
When the earth drinks the Current and the stones forget their songs,
Then shall the Primal One emerge from the Hollow,
Bearing the First Howl in their throat,
And the Second Song shall begin."

Origin

This prophecy emerged in 700 YFH, attributed to a blind Listener named Sera the Silent. Sera was not a Wizard. She was a pre-Howl shaman who had survived the evolutionary surge without gaining magical ability. She claimed to hear the future in the wind.

The prophecy was ignored for centuries. It was considered "pre-Howl superstition" by the Orders. It survived only in oral tradition among the Outlands tribes.

The Great Silence Connection

In 1,100 YFH, when the Great Silence occurred, dogs across Pawtheon remembered Sera's prophecy. The Silent Ones (the Mute) had risen. The hats had gone dark. The earth had drunk the Current. The stones had forgotten their songs.

But the Primal One did not emerge from the Hollow. The Gray Dog remained on the unnamed mountain. The Second Song did not begin.

The prophecy was partially fulfilled. But partially fulfilled prophecies are more dangerous than unfulfilled ones.

Interpretations

The Chorus Interpretation: The prophecy was metaphorical. The "Silent Ones" were not the Mute. They were the ordinary dogs who had always been silent in the shadow of the Wizards. The "Second Song" was not a new First Howl. It was the renewed appreciation for magic after the Silence. The prophecy predicted the Great Silence and its aftermath, not a future cataclysm.

The Mute Interpretation: The prophecy was literal but incomplete. The Great Silence was the first phase. The Second Song will begin when the Hollow returns permanently. The Primal One who emerges will not be the Gray Dog. It will be a new dog, born from the silence, who will end magic forever.

The Deep Order Interpretation: The prophecy was a probability, not a certainty. Sera saw a likely future, not an inevitable one. The Great Silence was one branch of a tree with many branches. The Second Song may still occur, or it may not. The Tidewatch has identified seventeen possible futures that branch from the Great Silence. The Second Song appears in four of them.

The Arcane Order Interpretation: The prophecy was a time-loop. Sera did not see the future. She remembered the past. The "Second Song" had already happened in a previous cycle of time, and Sera's "prophecy" was actually a memory of that cycle bleeding through. The Looped sect uses this interpretation as evidence for their circular-time theory.

II. THE WRONG NOTE PROPHECY

The Prophecy

"A Wizard shall hear the Wrong Note in the heart of the empire.
The Wrong Note shall be the first warning.
Three shall hear: the Flame, the Deep, and the Dream.
Three shall act: the Flame, the Deep, and the Dream.
But three are not enough.
The Primal One must howl again, or the song ends."

Origin

This prophecy emerged in 900 YFH, attributed to a Deep Wizard named Pool-Seer the Half-Blind. Pool-Seer claimed to have heard the prophecy while scrying in a polluted pool — a pool contaminated by Mud Rot residue from the Shadowmire.

The Tidewatch initially dismissed the prophecy as "rot-induced hallucination." But Pool-Seer wrote it down, sealed it in wax, and instructed that it be opened only after his death. He died in 910 YFH. The prophecy was opened. Two years later, the first "Wrong Note" was reported.

The Wrong Note

The Wrong Note is a frequency that should not exist. It is a sound that occurs when a resonant structure — a Tower, a hat, a singing stone — produces a tone that does not match its moon's frequency.

The first reported Wrong Note occurred in the Forge Spire in 912 YFH. A furnace produced a tone that was not Emberhowl's frequency. It was lower. Deeper. Almost... hollow.

The furnace was repaired. The note stopped. But similar reports have occurred sporadically ever since: in the Solar Spire's light-towers, in the Tidewatch's scrying pools, in the Dream Spire's sleep-chambers.

Interpretations

The Chorus Interpretation: The Wrong Note is a natural phenomenon. Resonant structures degrade over time. A degraded structure produces off-frequency tones. The prophecy was a lucky guess based on Pool-Seer's knowledge of acoustic engineering.

The Deep Order Interpretation: The Wrong Note is a genuine warning. The Tidewatch has mapped all reported Wrong Notes and found that they cluster around ley-line intersections. The Wrong Note appears when a ley-line is "stressed" — when too much magical energy flows through it. The prophecy predicts a future ley-line collapse that could devastate Pawtheon.

The Mute Interpretation: The Wrong Note is the Hollow's voice. The Hollow is trying to speak through the resonant structures. The prophecy predicts that the Hollow will eventually succeed, and the Wrong Note will become the Only Note.

The Gray Dog's Interpretation: When asked about the Wrong Note, the Gray Dog reportedly said: "A wrong note is still a note. Silence is the only true wrongness."

III. THE PACK-BREAKER PROPHECY

The Prophecy

"When the pack turns on itself,
When the loyal bite the loyal,
When the Current is used not to build but to destroy,
Then shall the Loyalty Network shiver and crack.
And from the crack, a new pack shall emerge,
Not born of blood or moon,
But of choice."

Origin

This prophecy emerged in 1,020 YFH, attributed to a Dream Wizard named Thread-Weaver the Fractured. Thread-Weaver was driven insane by a failed dream-thread healing. She spent the last ten years of her life in the Dream Spire's care ward, speaking only in prophecies.

The Pack-Breaker prophecy was recorded by her caregivers. It was ignored until the Loyalty Wars began in 1,145 YFH.

Fulfillment

The Loyalty Wars fulfilled the first three lines perfectly. The pack turned on itself. The loyal bit the loyal. The Current was used to destroy.

The Loyalty Network did shiver. Dogs across Pawtheon reported feeling "wrong" during the Wars — a sensation of disconnection, as if the Network itself was in pain.

But the "new pack" did not emerge. Not yet.

The Mute believes they are the new pack. The Chorus believes the post-War reconciliation was the new pack. The Arcane Order believes the new pack has not yet formed.

Interpretations

The Chorus Interpretation: The prophecy was fulfilled by the Loyalty Accords. The "new pack" was not a physical pack but a philosophical one: the six Orders, bound by treaty rather than by blood or magic.

The Mute Interpretation: The prophecy predicts the Mute's eventual triumph. The "new pack" will be dogs who choose loyalty without magic. The Loyalty Wars were merely the first crack. The full breaking is still to come.

The Dream Order Interpretation: The "new pack" will be born from the fractured dream-threads of the Wars. Somewhere in the collective unconscious, a new consciousness is forming — a pack-mind that exists only in dreams. When it is strong enough, it will wake up.

The Gray Dog's Interpretation: The Gray Dog, when asked, reportedly said: "The new pack is already here. You are simply looking for it in the wrong places."

IV. NATURAL OMENS

The Singing Stone Crack

When a personal singing stone — a resonant crystal that many dogs carry for meditation — cracks without cause, it is considered an omen of great change. The change may be personal (death, transformation) or collective (war, revolution).

The Chorus considers this superstition. The Deep Order has documented 347 cases of singing stone cracking, of which 198 were followed by significant events within one year. The correlation is statistically significant but causally unexplained.

The Howling Winds

In the Frosthollow Expanse, sudden shifts in wind frequency are believed to be messages from the dead. When the wind howls at a specific off-frequency tone — lower than Emberhowl, higher than Deepwell — it is called the Hollow Wind and considered an omen of approaching silence.

The Tidewatch has recorded Hollow Wind events. They occur approximately once per decade. They do not correlate with any known astronomical or meteorological pattern.

The Mud Rot Bubble

In the Shadowmire, the Great Rot occasionally produces bubbles of gas that rise from the stagnant water and burst with a sound like a distant howl. Local Zombies interpret these bubbles as messages from the Rot itself. The messages are usually incomprehensible.

The Wild Order believes the bubbles are natural decomposition. The Rot Gardeners believe they are communications. The Mute believes they are the Hollow's voice, filtered through corruption.

The Moon-Dog

A rare astronomical event: when a dog-shaped cloud passes in front of a full moon, casting a shadow that resembles a howling dog. This is considered the most auspicious omen in Pawtheon.

The last Moon-Dog was seen in 1,150 YFH, three days before the Gray Dog intervened in the Loyalty Wars. The one before that was in 1,000 YFH, during the Millennium Concordance.

V. HUMAN OMENS

The Sudden Mute

A dog who loses the ability to channel magic overnight — not gradually, as in renunciation, but suddenly, as if a switch has been flipped — is considered an omen. The Sudden Mute may be a warning of a future Great Silence, or they may be a sign that the Hollow is reaching for a specific dog.

Approximately 20 Sudden Mutes have been recorded since the Great Silence. Most occurred within one year of the event. The rate has decreased since then, but it has not stopped.

The Frequency Dream

Some dogs report dreams in which they hear a frequency they have never encountered in waking life. These dreams are sometimes prophetic. Sometimes they inspire new magical techniques.

The Dream Order collects frequency dreams. Their archive contains 12,000 recorded dreams, of which 400 were followed by verifiable prophecies or inventions.

The Pup Who Hums

Pups who hum before they howl are considered auspicious. In the post-Silence era, they are also considered dangerous. The Chorus Council monitors such pups closely, believing they may be sensitive to frequencies that other dogs cannot perceive.

The Mute recruits pups who hum. They believe humming is a sign of natural musicality that does not require magical enhancement.
