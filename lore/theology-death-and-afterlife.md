## EDITOR'S NOTE
*From the Tower Archive, Mortological Folio, compiled by Sister Deep-Current, Tidewatch, 1,100 YFH*

Death in Pawtheon is complicated by magic. A dog who dies while channeling the Current leaves a residue. A dog who dies with a dream-thread intact leaves an echo. A dog who dies in the Shadowmire may not stay dead.

[Ed. Note] I have recorded what each tradition believes, what each Order practices, and what actually happens. The three are not always the same.

## I. THE CHORUS ORTHODOXY

### Belief

When a dog dies, their magic returns to the moon that fed them. The body is temporary. The Current is eternal.

A Flame Wizard's fire does not go out when they die. It returns to Emberhowl, where it is redistributed among the living Flame Wizards. A Radiant Wizard's light returns to Solaris. A Deep Wizard's visions return to Deepwell. And so on for all six Orders.

The Wanderer is the exception. His magic does not return to any moon. It returns to the unnamed mountain, where it seeps into the stone and waits. Some Deep Wizards believe this is why the mountain hums at frequencies no instrument can measure.

### Practice

**The Hat Return:** When a Wizard dies, their hat is brought to their Order's Tower and placed on a Hook of Return — a resonant peg tuned to the moon's frequency. The hat sits there for one full moon cycle, allowing the magic to drain back to its source. After the cycle, the hat is burned. The ash is scattered on the Tower's foundation.

**The Current Wake:** For three days after death, the deceased's packmates (or Order-mates) gather and channel the Current together. This is not a funeral. It is a redistribution. The collective channeling helps the dead dog's magic find its way home.

**The Silent Day:** On the seventh day after death, no one channels magic. The Order observes a day of silence, acknowledging that the dead dog's share of the Current has been absorbed and the world has moved on.

### Criticism

The Mute rejects the Hat Return entirely. "You burn the chain and scatter the ash," they say. "But the dog is still dead. The moon still feeds on their memory."

The Chorus responds: "The moon does not feed on memory. The moon feeds the living. The dead dog's magic becomes another dog's flame. This is not consumption. It is continuation."

## II. THE OLD PACK WAY: THE LONG RUN

### Belief

Before the First Howl, dogs believed in the Long Run: an endless plain where dead dogs ran forever, never tiring, never hungry, surrounded by their pack.

The Old Pack Way did not disappear after magic arrived. It was adapted. Modern believers — mostly in Frosthollow and the Deepwood — say that the Long Run still exists, but it has been upgraded. The dead dogs do not just run. They howl. They howl the First Howl over and over, eternally, sustaining the Current from the other side.

### Practice

**The Scent-Trail:** Still practiced in isolated packs. When a dog dies, the pack lays a scent-trail from the body to the highest point they can reach, guiding the spirit to the Long Run.

**The Circle Howl for the Dead:** The pack forms a circle around the body and howls in rotation, each dog taking one note. The chord rises and falls. The belief is that the chord opens a door to the Long Run.

[Pack Memory] Some Wizards report that performing the Circle Howl for the dead produces a measurable spike in the Loyalty Network. This has never been verified.

## III. REALM-SPECIFIC DEATH TRADITIONS

### Frosthollow

The Frosthollow Huskies believe that cold preserves the soul. When a dog dies, the body is left on the ice for three days, allowing the spirit to "freeze" into the landscape. After three days, the body is burned. The smoke is believed to carry the frozen soul to the aurora, where it becomes part of the northern lights.

**The Ice Speakers:** A sub-tradition holds that the aurora is the collective voice of all dead Frosthollow dogs. On clear nights, Listeners (pre-Howl shamans) still sit on the ice and try to hear messages from the dead in the shifting lights.

### Ember Wastes

The Tigers believe in consumption by fire. A dead dog is placed on a pyre of volcanic stone and burned. The ash is mixed with volcanic glass and shaped into a small orb. The orb is kept by the pack and struck against stone in times of need, producing a tone that is believed to summon the dead dog's courage.

**The Ash Orbs:** Over centuries, some packs have accumulated hundreds of ash orbs. The oldest orbs no longer produce sound — the ash has compacted too densely. These "silent orbs" are considered the most sacred, representing dogs so ancient that even their echoes have faded.

### Sunward Heights

The Solids believe in dissolution by light. A dead dog is placed on the Purified Plateau at noon, where the sun burns away the body over several days. What remains is a white stain on the stone — a "sun-print" that marks where the dog stood.

**The Plateau of Prints:** The Purified Plateau is covered in thousands of white stains. Solids walk among them in silence, believing that the dead are still present in the light that reflects off the stone.

### Deepwood

The Collies believe in root-burial. A dead dog is buried at the base of a tree, and the tree's roots are encouraged to grow through the body. Over decades, the tree absorbs the dog's nutrients and grows stronger. The Collies believe the dog becomes part of the forest's memory.

**The Memory Trees:** Old trees in the Deepwood are said to contain the memories of every dog buried at their roots. The Collie Wardens — a Wild Order sub-order — claim to hear these memories as whispers in the leaves.

### Abyssal Reaches

The Splits believe in water-division. A dead dog is placed in a boat and set adrift on the divide between fresh and salt water. The current carries the boat until it sinks. The Splits believe the dead dog's spirit splits in two: one half flows to the sea, one half returns to the land. Both halves are at peace, because the Split nature is preserved even in death.

**The Twin Currents:** On the anniversary of a death, the family releases two boats: one upstream, one downstream. Whichever boat sinks first is believed to indicate which half of the spirit was stronger.

### Violet Highlands

The Classics believe in record-keeping. A dead dog's life is written on a stone tablet and placed in the Archivum's Hall of the Dead. The tablet includes the dog's name, breed, achievements, and — most importantly — their final words.

**The Speaking Stones:** Some stones in the Hall of the Dead are tuned to faint frequencies. The Arcane Order believes these are natural resonances. The Classics believe they are the dead dogs' voices, preserved in stone.

### Shadowmire

The Zombies have no death tradition because, technically, they do not die. A Zombie dog who is "killed" simply stops moving until the rot reanimates them. This can take hours or years. Zombie families keep their "dead" in the Zombie Crypts, waiting for reanimation.

**The Crypt Keepers:** A Shadowmire tradition holds that a Zombie who has been "dead" for more than a century is no longer the same dog. The reanimated body has a new consciousness, shaped by the rot. These "new dogs" are given new names and welcomed as family members. The old name is scratched off the crypt wall.

## IV. THE UNDEAD QUESTION

### What Is a Zombie?

The Shadowmire Zombies are the only dogs in Pawtheon who do not fit the standard model of death. They are not alive in the biological sense. They are not dead in the spiritual sense. They exist in a state that the Violet Citadel calls "persistent animation."

The rot that sustains them is not a disease. It is a transformation. A Zombie dog's body continues to function — heart beating, blood flowing, brain active — despite tissue degradation that would kill any other dog. The rot replaces what decays. It is symbiotic, not parasitic.

### Death for a Zombie

A Zombie dog can be "killed" by destroying the brain or severing the spine. But even then, the body may reanimate if the rot is strong enough. True death for a Zombie requires complete incineration or dissolution in acid.

The Zombie dogs themselves do not fear death. They fear "forgetting" — the state where reanimation produces a new consciousness with no memory of the previous life. To a Zombie, forgetting is the true death.

### The Mute and Zombies

The Mute has a complex relationship with Zombie dogs. On one hand, the Mute rejects magic, and Zombie existence is sustained by a form of magic (the Shadowmire's unique resonance with rot). On the other hand, the Mute respects the Zombies' acceptance of decay as natural. "They do not pretend to be eternal," Ashpaw wrote. "They are honest about what they are."

## V. THE DREAM ECHO

### Belief

The Dream Order believes that dogs who die with strong dream-threads leave an Echo: a persistent oneiroic imprint that can be visited by living Dream Wizards.

An Echo is not a ghost. It is not the dead dog's soul. It is a memory so intense that it has become self-sustaining, like a song that continues to hum after the singer has left the room.

### Practice

**The Dream Walk:** A Dream Wizard enters a controlled sleep and searches for the deceased's Echo in the collective unconscious. If they find it, they can speak with it. The Echo knows everything the dead dog knew at the moment of death, but nothing after.

**The Fade:** Echoes do not last forever. Over time, they dissolve into the general dream-noise of the world. The Dream Order estimates that most Echoes fade within fifty years. Very strong ones — usually belonging to powerful Wizards or dogs with exceptional emotional intensity — may last centuries.

**The Gray Dog's Echo:** No Dream Wizard has ever found the Gray Dog's Echo. This is either because he has not died, or because his dream-threads are so powerful that they exist on a frequency no sleeping mind can reach.

## VI. THE GREAT SILENCE'S DEAD

### The Unfinished Death

The Great Silence killed thousands of dogs. But it killed them strangely.

Dogs who died during the Silence — whether from cold, hunger, or violence — left no magical residue. Their hats did not return to the moons. Their dream-threads did not leave Echoes. Their bodies did not produce the usual oneiroic imprint.

The Deep Order calls these "unfinished deaths." The dogs died, but their magic did not know where to go. The moons were dark. The Current was still. The magic simply... stopped.

Some Tidewatch seers believe that the unfinished dead are still present, not as ghosts but as absences. Places where many dogs died during the Silence are said to be "quiet" in a way that has nothing to do with sound. Dogs who enter these places report feeling "less than themselves."

The Mute considers these places sacred.
