PRE-HOWL HISTORY

The Archaeological Layer Beneath the Magic

[Edition note] Revised1200YFH edition. Original compiler dates below identify earlier textual layers; later events and citations belong to this revision. Canon Contract continuity settlement governs literal chronology, seat counts and physical rules.

EDITOR'S NOTE

From the Tower Archive, Archaeological Folio, compiled by Brother Violet-Deep, 1,030 YFH

The world did not begin with the First Howl. The dogs did not spring into existence as magical beings. There was a before. It is buried. It is forgotten. But it is not gone.

[Ed. Note] Everything in this document is inferred. There are no written records from before the First Howl. There are only bones, stones, and the occasional pack-memory fragment that predates the Current.

I have tried to be honest about what is proven, what is likely, and what is wishful thinking.

--- Brother Violet-Deep

I. THE ORDINARY WORLD

The Physical Landscape

The Pawtheon of 2,000 PH was geographically identical to the Pawtheon of today. The seven realms existed. The seven moons hung in the sky. The unnamed mountain rose at the center of the world.

What was different was the dogs. They were smaller. They walked on four legs. They had no magic. They were, by every measure, ordinary canids.

The Climate

The pre-Howl climate was slightly cooler. Frosthollow's glaciers extended further south. The Ember Wastes were less volcanic. The Deepwood was smaller. The Shadowmire was a swamp, not a rotting wasteland.

Archaeological evidence suggests that the First Howl itself caused climatic changes. The sudden influx of magical energy warmed the world. The Ember Wastes became more active. The Shadowmire began to rot.

The Fauna

Pre-Howl Pawtheon had prey animals that no longer exist: large grazers with thick hides, fast runners with antlers, burrowing creatures that lived in the Deepwood's root systems. Most of these species went extinct during the First Howl's evolutionary surge. The dogs outcompeted them.

A few species survived in isolated populations. The Frosthollow ice-wolves — a non-magical canid species — still exist in the far north. They are considered a living link to the pre-Howl world.

II. PRE-HOWL SOCIETY

Pack Structure

Pre-Howl dogs lived in packs of 10–30 individuals. Packs were territorial but not hostile. Border disputes were resolved through ritualized howling competitions, not violence.

The pack leader — always the oldest female — was called the Mother. She was not a dictator. She was a mediator. Her authority came from experience, not force.

The Extended Network

Packs were not isolated. They maintained connections through a system called the Scent-Web: a network of scent-marked paths that allowed packs to communicate across vast distances. A pack in Frosthollow could know what a pack in the Ember Wastes was experiencing, not through magic but through the gradual transmission of scent-messages along the Web.

The Scent-Web is the direct ancestor of the Loyalty Network. The magical version is faster and more precise. But the pre-Howl version was older, more resilient, and required no magic to maintain.

Trade and Exchange

Pre-Howl dogs traded. Not with currency — they had none — but with favors. A Frosthollow pack might send a load of ice-preserved meat to a Sunward Heights pack in exchange for dried grasses. The exchange was mediated by the Scent-Web: each pack maintained a mental ledger of who owed what.

Some archaeologists believe this "favor economy" is the origin of the modern concept of loyalty. A dog who owed a favor was "bound" to the pack who had helped them. The binding was not magical. It was social.

III. PRE-HOWL SPIRITUALITY

The Silent Gods

The pre-Howl dogs did not worship the moons as sources of power. They worshipped them as witnesses. The Silent Gods — the pre-Howl name for the seven moons — were believed to have been watching since before dogs existed.

The core belief was simple: "The Silent Gods see everything. Therefore, we must be worthy of being seen."

This was not a theology of reward and punishment. The Silent Gods did not intervene. They did not answer prayers. They simply watched. And the dogs' response was to live as if they were always being observed.

The Listeners

Some dogs claimed to hear whispers from the Silent Gods. These were the Listeners: dogs with unusually acute hearing who could detect patterns in wind, water, and stone that others missed.

The Listeners were not priests. They were scouts. Their role was to interpret natural signs and advise the pack. A Listener who predicted a storm was valued. A Listener who claimed the Silent Gods were speaking directly to them was usually ignored.

The Long Run

The pre-Howl afterlife was called the Long Run: an endless plain where dead dogs ran forever, surrounded by their pack. The belief was not that the Long Run was "real" in a literal sense. It was that the Long Run was what loyalty looked like when it was no longer constrained by bodies.

The pre-Howl dogs did not fear death. They feared being alone in death. A dog who died without a pack — an exile, a wanderer, a dog who had been cast out — was believed to run at the edge of the Long Run, forever chasing a pack that never slowed down.

Rituals

The Circle Howl: When a pup was born, the pack formed a circle and howled in rotation, each dog taking one note. The chord rose and fell. It was welcome, not worship.

The Moon-Watch: The oldest dog in the pack sat awake while the others slept, looking up at the Silent Gods. When the Moon-Watcher died, the pack held a silent vigil.

The Scent-Trail to the Dead: When a dog died, the pack laid a scent-trail from the body to the highest point they could reach, guiding the spirit to the Long Run.

The Bone Niche: The skulls of past pack leaders were preserved in a hollow, not as trophies but as reminders. The niche was a place for the dead to remain part of the conversation.

IV. THE FIRST SIGNS OF CHANGE

The Dreaming Dogs

Approximately 50 years before the First Howl, some dogs began reporting unusual dreams. They dreamed of standing on two legs. They dreamed of manipulating objects with their paws. They dreamed of the moons singing.

The Listeners interpreted these dreams as warnings. The Silent Gods were restless, they said. Something was about to change.

Other dogs interpreted the dreams as illness. The dreaming dogs were isolated. Some were killed. Some were driven out.

Archaeological evidence suggests that the first "dreaming dogs" were actually the first dogs to feel the Current — not strongly enough to shape it, but strongly enough to sense it in their sleep.

The Gray Dog's Arrival

The Gray Dog appeared approximately 10 years before the First Howl. He was not recognized as special. He was simply a gray dog with round glasses who sat on the unnamed mountain and watched.

Pre-Howl dogs who encountered him reported that he was "patient" and "sad." He did not hunt. He did not join a pack. He simply sat.

Some Listeners claimed to hear him humming — a low, continuous tone that did not match any natural frequency. Other dogs heard nothing.

The Gray Dog sat for ten years. Then he opened his mouth and howled.

V. THE FIRST HOWL'S IMPACT

Immediate Effects (0–50 YFH)

The First Howl did not instantly transform the world. Its effects were gradual:

  • 0–10 YFH: Dogs within 100 leagues of the unnamed mountain reported feeling "different." Their senses were sharper. Their bonds with packmates were stronger.
  • 10–20 YFH: The first dogs stood on two legs. It was clumsy. It was painful. But it was useful for manipulating objects.
  • 20–30 YFH: The first deliberate magic: a dog in the Ember Wastes created fire without flint. A dog in Frosthollow warmed a den without body heat. A dog in the Deepwood accelerated a plant's growth.
  • 30–50 YFH: The first magical communities formed. The first conflicts. The first realization that magic without structure was dangerous.

Evolutionary Changes (50–200 YFH)

The dogs changed physically:

  • They grew larger.
  • Their paws became more dexterous.
  • Their brains expanded.
  • Their lifespans increased from approximately 15 years to approximately 50 years.

These changes were not uniform. Some dogs evolved faster than others. Some dogs did not evolve at all. The non-evolving dogs — the "ordinaries" — were not persecuted. They were simply left behind.

Cultural Changes (200–400 YFH)

The pre-Howl culture was gradually replaced:

  • The Scent-Web was superseded by the Loyalty Network.
  • The favor economy was superseded by a currency system (the first coins appeared in 300 YFH).
  • The Circle Howl was preserved but reinterpreted as a magical ritual.
  • The Bone Niche was abandoned in most packs, replaced by the Hat Return.
  • The Long Run was preserved but modified: now it was believed that dead dogs ran AND howled, sustaining the Current from the other side.

VI. PRE-HOWL RUINS

The Scent-Web Paths

The old scent-marked paths are still visible in some places. They appear as worn grooves in stone, too regular to be natural. Dogs who walk them report feeling "connected" in a way that has nothing to do with magic.

The Pilgrim Packs have begun using the old Scent-Web paths for their sacred routes. They believe the paths remember the pre-Howl dogs who walked them.

The Bone Niches

Caves with natural or carved hollows containing dog skulls have been found in every realm. The skulls show no tool marks, suggesting they were cleaned by scavengers rather than processed by the pack.

The Mute has adopted the bone niche as part of their practice. They consider it "the only honest tradition" because it predates magic.

The Listener Posts

Worn stone in caves and tree-hollows marks where pre-Howl Listeners sat for their seven-day vigils. Some modern Listeners claim to hear "echoes" at these posts — not magical echoes, but something older.

The First Tower Foundation

Beneath the Forge Spire, archaeologists have found the foundation of a much older structure: a stone circle, approximately 50 feet in diameter, with seven pillars arranged in a pattern that matches the moons' orbital positions.

The structure predates the First Howl by at least 10,000 years. Who built it? No one knows. The pre-Howl dogs did not have the tools or the organization. The Arcane Order has proposed — quietly — that the structure was built by a pre-dog civilization. There is no evidence for this. But there is also no evidence against it.

The Gray Dog, when asked about the stone circle, reportedly said: "I sat there for a long time."

VII. THE UNCHANGED

Some things in Pawtheon have not changed since the pre-Howl era:

  • Pack loyalty: The fundamental social unit is still the pack. The Orders are simply large, specialized packs.
  • The howl: Dogs still howl. The howl is still the primary form of long-distance communication.
  • Scent-marking: Even Wizards scent-mark their territory. It is instinctive.
  • The hunt: Dogs still hunt. Even Wizards who can create food with magic prefer to hunt. It is not about sustenance. It is about what the hunt means.
  • The circle: Dogs still sleep in circles, bodies touching, for warmth and protection. Even in Towers, Wizards sleep in circular arrangements.
