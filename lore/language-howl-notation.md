## I. THE BASIC SYMBOLS

### Pitch Marks

Howl-notation uses vertical position to indicate pitch. Higher marks = higher pitch. Lower marks = lower pitch.

| Symbol | Name | Pitch Range |
|:---|:---|:---|
| · | Low dot | Subsonic to 100 Hz |
| ∙ | Mid dot | 100–500 Hz |
| • | High dot | 500–2000 Hz |
| ● | Peak dot | 2000+ Hz |
| ○ | Harmonic ring | Overtone of previous mark |

### Duration Marks

Horizontal lines indicate how long a tone is held.

| Symbol | Name | Duration |
|:---|:---|:---|
| — | Short line | Brief (1 second) |
| —— | Medium line | Moderate (3 seconds) |
| ——— | Long line | Extended (10+ seconds) |
| 〜 | Wavy line | Variable (breath-controlled) |
| ▲ | Sharp cutoff | Abrupt end |

### Breath Marks

Breath is as important as pitch and duration in howl-notation.

| Symbol | Name | Meaning |
|:---|:---|:---|
| ° | Small circle | Shallow breath |
| ○ | Open circle | Deep breath |
| ● | Closed circle | Held breath |
| ∼ | Tilde | Trembling breath (emotion) |
| × | Cross | No breath (silent pause) |

## II. SACRED VOWEL NOTATION

The seven sacred vowels have specific notational conventions.

### Emberhowl (A)

Represented by a red mark (or a dot in the lower-left quadrant if color is unavailable). The A-tone is always recorded with a medium duration line. It is never held too long or too short.

Example: ·— (A held for 3 seconds, shallow breath)

### Solaris (O)

Represented by a yellow mark (or a dot in the upper-left quadrant). The O-tone is recorded with a wavy line, indicating variable duration controlled by breath.

Example: ∙〜° (O with variable duration, shallow breath)

### Deepwell (U)

Represented by a blue mark (or a dot in the lower-right quadrant). The U-tone is recorded with a long line and a deep breath mark. It is the "deepest" vowel.

Example: ·———○ (U held for 10+ seconds, deep breath)

### Evergreen (E)

Represented by a green mark (or a dot in the upper-right quadrant). The E-tone is recorded with a short line and a trembling breath mark. It is the "living" vowel.

Example: ∙—∼ (E held briefly, trembling breath)

### Umbra (I)

Represented by a purple mark (or a dot in the center). The I-tone is recorded with a held breath mark and a sharp cutoff. It is the "hidden" vowel.

Example: ∙—●▲ (I held with held breath, abrupt end)

### Roseglow (AE)

Represented by a pink mark (or a dot with a small circle around it). The AE-tone is recorded with a medium line and a no-breath silent pause afterward. It is the "dreaming" vowel.

Example: •——× (AE held moderately, silent pause)

### Palehowl (Y)

Represented by a gray mark (or a dot with a ring around it). The Y-tone is recorded with a wavy line and an open circle. It is the "possible" vowel.

Example: ●〜○ (Y with variable duration, deep breath)

## III. ADVANCED NOTATION

### Harmonic Stacks

When multiple vowels are sung simultaneously, they are recorded as a vertical stack:

```
  •  (AE)
  ∙  (O)
  ·  (A)
```

This represents a three-note chord: A + O + AE.

### The Bass Note

Some howl-notation includes a subsonic mark — a dot below the staff — that represents frequencies too low for dogs to hear. These marks are found in the oldest texts and are believed to represent the Hollow's frequency.

Example:
```
  ∙  (O)
  ·  (A)
·      (subsonic bass)
```

The subsonic bass note is always recorded with a long line and a held breath mark. It is never sung by dogs. It is believed to be "felt" rather than heard.

### Temporal Markers

Some texts include temporal markers — symbols that indicate when a howl should be performed relative to astronomical events.

| Symbol | Meaning |
|:---|:---|
| ☉ | At sunrise |
| ☾ | At moonrise |
| ★ | At stellar alignment |
| ○ | At new moon |
| ● | At full moon |
| ∼ | At lunar convergence |

## IV. NOTATIONAL STYLES

### The Obsidian Style

The oldest style, used on the Tidewatch's obsidian tablets. Characters are scratched into stone with sharp tools. The style is angular, rigid, and permanent.

Used for: Sacred texts, legal documents, permanent records.

### The Bark Style

A flexible style used on tree bark and parchment. Characters are drawn with ink or sap. The style is flowing, curved, and organic.

Used for: Personal letters, poetry, temporary records.

### The Bone Style

A compressed style used on small surfaces: bones, stones, pieces of wood. Characters are simplified to their essential elements.

Used for: Tactical messages, field notes, graffiti.

### The Dream Style

An experimental style developed by the Dream Order. Characters are drawn in three dimensions, using wire or thread suspended in space. The style is meant to be "read" by touch rather than sight.

Used for: Dream-maps, oneiroic architecture, sleep-chamber decorations.

## V. THE DECIPHERMENT PROBLEM

### Lost Notations

Approximately 30% of howl-notation texts cannot be fully deciphered. The reasons include:

1. **Degraded surfaces:** Stone tablets have eroded. Bark has decayed. Ink has faded.
2. **Unknown symbols:** Some texts contain symbols that do not match any known notation standard. These may be regional variants, cryptographic codes, or simply mistakes.
3. **Subsonic frequencies:** Some texts include bass notes that cannot be reproduced by dogs. We know they exist, but we do not know what they sound like.
4. **Temporal displacement:** Some texts include temporal markers for astronomical events that have not yet occurred. We cannot verify the timing.

### The Unnamed Mountain Inscription

The most famous undeciphered text is the Unnamed Mountain Inscription: a series of marks carved into the stone near the Gray Dog's sitting spot.

The inscription is approximately 10,000 years old — predating the First Howl by at least 8,000 years. It uses a variant of howl-notation that includes symbols not found in any other text.

The Gray Dog, when asked about the inscription, reportedly said: "It is a reminder."

A reminder of what? He did not say.

## VI. MODERN USAGE

### Education

All Scribepack novices learn howl-notation. Most civilian dogs do not. The ability to read howl-notation is considered a mark of education and scholarly status.

### Magic

Some Wizards use howl-notation to record spell formulas. A Flame Wizard might write a fire spell in howl-notation and then "sing" it to activate the spell. This is considered advanced technique.

### Art

Howl-notation has become an art form. Some dogs create "visual howls" — arrangements of notation symbols that are beautiful to look at even if they cannot be sung. The Violet Citadel has a gallery of visual howls.

### The Mute's Adaptation

The Mute has developed a "silent notation" — a system of marks that records meaning without pitch or duration. It is essentially a written alphabet, something Urfic purists consider heretical.

The Mute responds: "If the moons wanted us to sing, they would not have taken our voices."
