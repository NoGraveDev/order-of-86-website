## I. THE ROOTS OF WAR

### The Great Silence's Aftermath (1,100–1,140 YFH)

The Great Silence did not just kill magic for forty-nine hours. It killed certainty.

Before the Silence, the Orders believed the Current was eternal. The moons were eternal. Magic was as reliable as gravity. The Silence proved that none of this was true.

The response was panic. Each Order scrambled to secure its resources, its territory, and its future. What began as prudent preparation became paranoid hoarding.

### The Resource Crisis

The Silence damaged the land. Crops failed. Ley-lines shifted. Some magical resources — fire-crystals, growth-soil, scrying-water — became scarce.

The Flame Order and the Wild Order both claimed a volcanic valley that produced both fire-crystals and growth-soil. The dispute, which had been simmering since 920 YFH, reignited.

The Deep Order and the Arcane Order both claimed a network of caves that contained ancient howl-notation tablets. The Deep Order wanted to scry their meaning. The Arcane Order wanted to study their structure. Neither would share.

The Radiant Order and the Dream Order both claimed the right to heal the traumatized. The Radiant Order offered light-based therapy. The Dream Order offered dream-thread counseling. They competed for patients. They slandered each other's methods.

### The Mute Factor

Ashpaw's Mute faction grew from 200 to 2,000 members in the decade after the Silence. They recruited not just renounced Wizards but civilians who had never trusted magic.

The Orders saw the Mute as a threat. Not a military threat — the Mute had no army — but an ideological threat. If dogs stopped believing in magic, the Orders' authority would collapse.

The Flame Order banned Mute pamphlets. The Radiant Order arrested Mute speakers. The Deep Order spied on Mute camps. The Wild Order burned Mute meeting-houses. The Arcane Order hacked Mute communication networks. The Dream Order — characteristically — tried to "heal" Mute members by forcibly re-establishing their dream-threads.

The Mute did not fight back. They simply grew. And the Orders grew more frightened.

## II. THE SPARK

### The Ember Valley Incident (1,145 YFH)

A Flame Wizard named Forge-Grim and a Wild Wizard named Green-Heart met at the disputed volcanic valley to negotiate. Both were young. Both were angry. Both had lost friends during the Silence.

The negotiation lasted three hours. No agreement was reached. Forge-Grim accused Green-Heart of "stealing fire-crystals." Green-Heart accused Forge-Grim of "poisoning the growth-soil with ash."

The argument became magical. Forge-Grim created a fire-wall. Green-Heart grew a thorn-barrier. The fire-wall burned the thorns. The thorns released toxins that choked Forge-Grim.

Both Wizards died.

Their packs — Forge-Grim's Flame apprentice-pack and Green-Heart's Wild grove-keepers — arrived at the valley simultaneously. They found their leaders dead. They blamed each other.

The first battle of the Loyalty Wars lasted six hours. Twelve Wizards died. Forty civilians — farmers and miners who lived near the valley — were caught in the crossfire.

## III. THE WARS

### Phase 1: The Valley Wars (1,145–1,147 YFH)

The Flame Order and the Wild Order went to war.

It was not a declared war. There were no battle lines, no generals, no strategy. It was a series of skirmishes: Flame Wizards burning Wild groves, Wild Wizards growing choke-plants around Flame Towers.

The other Orders tried to stay neutral. They failed.

- **The Radiant Order** attempted to mediate. Both sides accused them of bias. Radiant healers who treated Flame wounded were attacked by Wild partisans. Radiant healers who treated Wild wounded were attacked by Flame partisans.

- **The Deep Order** tried to prophecy an end to the conflict. Their prophecies contradicted each other. Some predicted Flame victory. Some predicted Wild victory. Some predicted mutual destruction. The Tidewatch stopped releasing prophements to avoid making things worse.

- **The Arcane Order** sold weapons to both sides. Dimensional rift-bombs to the Flame Order. Temporal stasis traps to the Wild Order. The Arcane Order later claimed they were "studying the effects of magical warfare." No one believed them.

- **The Dream Order** attempted to use dream-threads to force empathy between Flame and Wild Wizards. The result was traumatic: Wizards who had killed each other's packmates were suddenly forced to feel their enemies' grief. Three Dream Wizards went insane. The Dream Order withdrew.

### Phase 2: The Expansion (1,147–1,149 YFH)

The war spread.

The Radiant Order, exhausted from failed mediation, declared that both the Flame and Wild Orders were "corrupted by violence" and announced a unilateral "purification campaign." Radiant Wizards began forcibly disarming combatants.

The Flame and Wild Orders, temporarily united by a common enemy, turned on the Radiant Order.

The Deep Order, fearing that the war would destroy all prophecy records, sealed the Tidewatch and declared neutrality. They were accused of cowardice by all sides.

The Arcane Order continued selling weapons to all three combatants.

The Dream Order retreated into the Dream Spire and refused to engage.

### Phase 3: The Burning of the Archivum (1,149 YFH)

The worst single event of the Wars.

A Flame Wizard named Fire-Rage — who had lost his entire pack in a Wild Order grove-fire — led a raid on the Archivum in the Violet Highlands. His goal was to destroy the Wild Order's historical records, which he believed contained evidence of "ancestral crimes."

The raid went wrong. Fire-Rage's flames spread beyond the target. The Archivum burned.

Eighty thousand documents were destroyed. Twenty Scribepack members died. Three hundred civilians died in the surrounding district.

The burning of the Archivum was the turning point. Even the most militant Wizards were shaken. The Archivum was sacred to all Orders. Its destruction was an attack on Pawtheon itself.

### Phase 4: The Gray Dog Intervenes (1,150 YFH)

The Gray Dog walked onto a battlefield.

It was not a dramatic entrance. He simply appeared one morning, walking between the Flame and Wild lines, and sat down.

Both sides had orders to fire. No one did.

He sat for three days. He did not eat. He did not drink. He did not speak. He simply sat.

On the fourth day, Fire-Rage — the arsonist of the Archivum — walked up to him and said: "Why are you stopping us?"

The Gray Dog looked at him. Then he opened his mouth and howled.

It was not the First Howl. It was not the Second Howl. It was a simple, ordinary howl. The kind any dog might make. But every Wizard on the battlefield felt it in their bones.

Fire-Rage dropped his weapon. He sat down. He cried.

The battle ended.

## IV. THE AFTERMATH

### The Loyalty Accords (1,155 YFH)

The Orders signed the Loyalty Accords five years after the Gray Dog's intervention. The Accords are simple:

1. No Order shall attack another Order.
2. Disputes shall be mediated by the Chorus Council.
3. No Order shall restrict another Order's access to shared resources.
4. No Order shall recruit from another Order's territory without permission.
5. The Archivum shall be rebuilt. All Orders shall contribute.
6. The Gray Dog's intervention shall be remembered. The details shall not be questioned.

The Accords have held for 45 years. They are the longest-lasting peace agreement in Pawtheon history.

The civilian casualties were caused by:
- Collateral damage from magical combat (45%)
- Famine due to destroyed farmland (30%)
- Disease due to disrupted sanitation magic (15%)
- Suicide due to trauma (10%)

### The War Criminals

Fire-Rage was arrested by the Chorus Guard. He was tried by a council of six Order leaders. He was found guilty of "crimes against Pawtheon" and sentenced to hat-seizure and exile.

He accepted the sentence calmly. Before his hat was burned, he said: "I was loyal to my pack. I forgot that all dogs are my pack."

He walked into the Shadowmire and was never seen again.

### The Rebuilding

The Archivum was rebuilt with contributions from all six Orders. It is now larger and more secure than before. The Scribepack has recovered approximately 30% of the lost documents through copies found in private collections and Mute camps.

The volcanic valley was divided in half, as the Chorus Council had proposed in 920 YFH. The Flame Order controls the northern half. The Wild Order controls the southern half. A neutral zone runs down the center, maintained by Tower-Keeps from both Orders.

The Mute, despite not participating in the Wars, offered to help rebuild. The Flame Order refused. The Wild Order accepted. This created a lasting bond between the Mute and the Wild Order that persists to this day.

## V. THE LESSONS

### What the Orders Learned

- **Flame:** "Control is not the same as strength. A fire that burns only enemies is not fire. It is a weapon."
- **Radiant:** "Light that blinds is not light. We tried to illuminate the conflict and burned everyone's eyes."
- **Deep:** "Prophecy without action is cowardice. We saw the war coming and did nothing."
- **Wild:** "Growth without boundaries is invasion. We grew our groves into Flame territory and called it natural."
- **Arcane:** "Knowledge without ethics is poison. We sold weapons and called it research."
- **Dream:** "Empathy without consent is assault. We forced feelings and called it healing."

### What the Civilians Learned

The civilian population learned that the Orders were not infallible. Before the Wars, Wizards were respected almost as demigods. After the Wars, they were respected as dogs — powerful, dangerous, flawed dogs.

The Mute's recruitment doubled again in the decade after the Wars. Not because civilians agreed with Ashpaw's doctrine. But because they had seen what magic could do when it was afraid.
